# Discussion Writing Workflow

Use this workflow after reading upstream artifacts when available.

## Required Inputs

- Key results.
- Evidence strength.
- Introduction gap or research question.
- Relevant literature or target-paper comparison points.
- Contribution candidates.
- Limitations.
- Future work or application scope.

## Upstream Handoff

From `content_analysis.md`, use:

- Contribution candidates.
- Experiment evidence and claim boundaries.
- Limitations, failed runs, and missing experiments.
- Baseline and ablation notes.

From `writing_blueprint.md`, use:

- Discussion move map.
- Literature-positioning strategy.
- Target-paper conventions for contribution, limitations, and future work.
- Terminology and certainty preferences.

From Results:

- Key findings.
- Figures/tables.
- Result issues.
- Comparison outcomes.

## Drafting Steps

1. Decide the opening.
   - Contribution-first, result-first, or context reboot.
   - Match target papers and evidence strength.

2. Interpret key results.
   - Revisit a result only to explain meaning.
   - Connect interpretation to the study aim.

3. Map to literature.
   - Compare agreement, disagreement, extension, or novelty.
   - Use citations only where they support a move.

4. State contribution.
   - Distinguish achievement inside the study from contribution to the field.
   - Preserve stable wording from upstream artifacts.

5. Discuss limitations.
   - Name limitations that affect interpretation or scope.
   - Link each to claim boundary or future work.

6. Identify implications or applications.
   - Keep them proportional to evidence.
   - Use modal language for potential uses.

7. Close with direction.
   - End by reinforcing contribution, application, or future path according to target convention.

## Deliverables

For an outline:

- Paragraph function.
- Results used.
- Literature link.
- Claim strength.
- Limitation/future work.

For a draft:

- Discussion text.
- Move map.
- Claim-risk notes.
- Missing citation or evidence needs.

For claim calibration:

- Original claim.
- Evidence.
- Risk.
- Recommended wording.
