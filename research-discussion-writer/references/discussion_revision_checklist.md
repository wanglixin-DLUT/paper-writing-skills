# Discussion Revision Checklist

Use this checklist before delivering a Discussion draft or revision.

## Structure

- Does the opening match target-paper conventions?
- Does the Discussion move beyond Results?
- Are key results interpreted in a logical order?
- Are limitations and future work placed where readers expect them?
- Does the ending leave a clear contribution, implication, or application?

## Evidence And Claims

- Is every interpretation supported by a result?
- Are mechanisms stated only when tested or clearly bounded?
- Are applications proportional to evidence?
- Are claims aligned with `content_analysis.md` and Results?
- Are uncertain claims softened?

## Literature

- Does each citation have a function?
- Does the Discussion explain agreement, contrast, extension, or unresolved disagreement?
- Are relevant Introduction citations revisited when needed?
- Are new citations introduced only when useful?

## Limitations

- Are limitations specific and meaningful?
- Are they linked to future work or claim boundaries?
- Are missing experiments or weak comparisons acknowledged when they matter?
- Do limitations avoid undermining supported contributions unnecessarily?

## Consistency

- Does the Discussion answer the Introduction gap?
- Does it match Abstract and Conclusion claims?
- Does it preserve terminology and contribution wording?
- Does it avoid repeating Results without interpretation?

## Final Checks

- No unsupported novelty claims.
- No overconfident causal language.
- No vague future-work ending.
- No hidden contradiction with Methods or Results.
- No invented citations.
