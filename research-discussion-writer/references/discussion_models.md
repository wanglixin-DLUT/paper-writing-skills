# Discussion Models

Use these structures as flexible menus. Target-paper conventions in `writing_blueprint.md` should guide the final order.

## Generic Discussion Move Menu

Common moves:

1. Announce the structure or content of the Discussion, if target papers do this.
2. State the achievement or contribution of the study.
3. Reboot the reader with relevant background, literature, gap, aim, or method.
4. Revisit key results and explore their implications.
5. Map results to existing literature or knowledge.
6. Explain agreements, disagreements, mechanisms, or unexpected findings.
7. Identify limitations, constraints, or uncertainties.
8. Suggest future work linked to those limitations or opportunities.
9. Restate contribution, impact, or applications.

The contribution may appear at the beginning, end, or throughout. Follow target papers and the strength of the evidence.

## Common Structures

### Contribution-First

Use when the main finding is strong and target papers open Discussion with impact.

Pattern:

- Main contribution or finding.
- Key result interpretation.
- Literature comparison.
- Limitations.
- Implications/applications.

### Result-By-Result

Use when several aims, hypotheses, or experiments each need interpretation.

Pattern per result:

- Key result.
- Meaning.
- Literature comparison.
- Limitation or boundary.

### Literature-Integrated

Use when contribution depends heavily on prior debates.

Pattern:

- Revisit research question.
- Compare result with prior positions.
- Explain what the current result changes.
- Bound the claim.

### Limitation-To-Future

Use when limitations are important and constructive.

Pattern:

- State limitation.
- Explain effect on interpretation.
- Identify what remains reliable.
- Suggest future work.

## Deep Learning Discussion Variant

Common elements:

- Why the proposed model or training strategy improved performance.
- What ablations reveal about each component.
- Where the method generalizes or fails.
- Efficiency, robustness, data, or deployment implications.
- Comparison with prior architectures, losses, or benchmarks.
- Limitations around datasets, compute, seeds, metric scope, or baseline fairness.

Do not claim mechanism if ablations do not isolate it.
