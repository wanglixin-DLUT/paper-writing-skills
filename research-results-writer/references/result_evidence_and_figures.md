# Result Evidence And Figures

Use this reference to keep Results traceable.

## Evidence Priority

Prefer:

1. User-confirmed final result tables.
2. `content_analysis.md` experiment and result map.
3. Logs and output files tied to configs and datasets.
4. Figures or tables already prepared for the paper.
5. Draft manuscript values.

Do not invent:

- Numeric results.
- Statistical significance.
- Baseline names or values.
- Dataset splits.
- Figure/table contents.
- Error bars or confidence intervals.

## Evidence Table

For each result claim, track:

- Claim:
- Metric:
- Value or trend:
- Comparator:
- Figure/table/log:
- Dataset/split:
- Reliability:
- Claim strength:
- Missing information:

## Figure And Table Narrative

When writing a visual callout:

- Name the figure/table.
- State what it displays.
- Direct attention to the key pattern.
- Give the number or comparison that matters.
- Explain enough context to prevent misreading.

Avoid:

- "Figure X shows the results" with no interpretation.
- Repeating every cell in a table.
- Introducing a figure before the reader knows why it matters.

## Selecting Results

Prioritize:

- Results that answer the main research question.
- Results that support the central contribution.
- Results that establish validity of the method.
- Baseline comparisons needed to interpret performance.
- Ablations that isolate the proposed component.
- Negative or limitation results that affect claims.

De-emphasize:

- Secondary runs that do not alter the claim.
- Exploratory results without clear provenance.
- Metrics not used by target papers or reviewers.

## Handling Problematic Results

Mention problems when:

- The result changes interpretation.
- A reviewer would notice it.
- It explains a limitation later.
- It affects comparison fairness.
- It bounds the contribution.

State the issue and its scope. Do not hide it, but do not let it dominate unless it is central.
