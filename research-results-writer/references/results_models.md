# Results Models

Use these structures as flexible menus. Target-paper conventions in `writing_blueprint.md` should guide the final order.

## Generic Results Move Menu

Common moves:

1. Revisit the aim, prediction, hypothesis, gap, or method.
   - Use only enough context for readers who jump directly to Results.
   - Reintroduce the method briefly if it is needed to understand a result.

2. Give a general result statement.
   - Stabilize the reader's frame before detailed numbers.
   - Use summary statements for a group of results.

3. Invite the reader to view a figure or table.
   - Point to the visual and name what it shows.
   - Do not assume the visual speaks for itself.

4. Present specific or key results.
   - Select the most important results for detail.
   - Include numbers, comparisons, and direction of effect where needed.

5. Compare results.
   - Compare with baselines, prior studies, predictions, simulations, model variants, or experimental conditions.

6. Explain results cautiously.
   - Use method details or known facts to explain why a result occurred.
   - Keep broader interpretation for Discussion unless target papers integrate it.

7. Report problems or issues.
   - Acknowledge incomplete, noisy, failed, negative, or unexpected results when they affect interpretation.

8. Mention possible implications.
   - Keep implications brief unless Results and Discussion are combined.

## Organization Options

- By research aim or hypothesis.
- By figure/table sequence.
- By experiment or study phase.
- By dataset or benchmark.
- By model component or ablation.
- By metric or evaluation dimension.
- By qualitative then quantitative evidence.
- By main result then supporting analyses.

## Deep Learning Results Variant

Common subsections:

- Main benchmark results.
- Comparison with baselines.
- Ablation studies.
- Sensitivity or robustness analysis.
- Efficiency, memory, latency, or compute.
- Qualitative examples or error analysis.
- Additional datasets or generalization.

Ensure each subsection connects to a contribution claim.

## Results And Discussion Combined

Use only when target-paper conventions support it or the user requests it.

When combined:

- Report result first.
- Explain or interpret immediately after.
- Keep claim strength visible.
- Avoid repeating the same interpretation again in a separate Discussion.

When separate:

- Keep Results focused on evidence and local explanation.
- Save broader meaning, limitations, implications, and future work for Discussion.
