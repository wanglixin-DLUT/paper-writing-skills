# Research Writing Reviser

Use this skill to revise scientific writing at the level of narrative, structure, sentence flow, meaning, terminology, and cross-section consistency.

This skill is part of the writing system:

- `content_analysis.md` supplies technical facts, evidence, limitations, and claim boundaries.
- `writing_blueprint.md` supplies reader strategy, section move maps, terminology decisions, and target-journal conventions.
- Section-specific drafts supply the text to diagnose or revise.

## Core Principle

Revision is not just polishing English. A manuscript can be grammatically correct and still fail if information has no clear function, sentences do not guide the reader, terminology drifts, contribution ownership is ambiguous, or claims exceed evidence.

Revise for reader control first, then sentence clarity, then local grammar and vocabulary.

## Start Here

1. Look for `content_analysis.md` and `writing_blueprint.md` in the active project/manuscript directory and nearby parent directories.
2. Identify the revision scope:
   - whole manuscript
   - one section
   - abstract/title package
   - response to reviewer
   - final pre-submission audit
3. If the user asks for a review, prioritize findings: bugs in logic, unsupported claims, missing evidence, structural problems, and high-impact clarity issues.
4. If editing text directly, preserve scientific meaning and flag uncertain changes.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose the document level.
   - Use `references/cross_section_audit.md`.
   - Check whether title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusion make the same promise and support it.

2. Diagnose information organization.
   - Use `references/organization_and_narrative.md`.
   - Ensure each paragraph and sentence has a function and leads the reader forward.

3. Diagnose sentence flow.
   - Use `references/sentence_flow.md`.
   - Check known-to-new order, sentence openings, links between sentences, overlong sentences, and dense noun phrases.

4. Diagnose grammar and vocabulary choices that affect meaning.
   - Use `references/grammar_vocabulary.md`.
   - Check tense, passive/active ownership, article use when meaningful, prepositions, pronoun reference, and stable terminology.

5. Apply revision.
   - Rewrite only as much as needed for the user's requested level.
   - Preserve technical terms unless they are inconsistent or undefined.
   - Mark missing citations, evidence, or project facts.

6. Deliver.
   - Provide revised text, prioritized findings, or an audit table depending on the request.
   - Explain high-impact changes briefly.

## Output Modes

- `diagnosis`: issue-focused review without rewriting everything.
- `revision`: revised text plus notes.
- `line edit`: sentence-level edits preserving structure.
- `structural edit`: paragraph and section reorganization.
- `pre-submission audit`: whole-paper consistency and claim check.
- `terminology audit`: consistency of terms, acronyms, and contribution language.
- `claim audit`: map claims to evidence and soften unsupported wording.

## Output Standards

A good revision:

- Improves the reader's path through the text.
- Makes each sentence's function clear.
- Preserves or clarifies scientific meaning.
- Keeps terminology stable.
- Clarifies who did what.
- Aligns claims with evidence.
- Flags missing citations, missing evidence, or unresolved ambiguity.
- Respects target-journal and target-paper conventions.
