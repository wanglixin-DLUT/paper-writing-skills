# Organization And Narrative

Use this reference to revise paragraphs, sections, and information order.

## Function First

For every paragraph and sentence, identify its function:

- establish importance
- provide background
- map prior work
- identify gap
- describe method
- report result
- interpret result
- compare with literature
- state implication
- acknowledge limitation
- propose future work

If a sentence has no function, remove or relocate it.

## Paragraph Control

Each paragraph should:

- Have one main function.
- Begin with a frame when the reader needs orientation.
- Develop in a logical order.
- End in a way that prepares the next paragraph.

Avoid:

- Whole-page paragraphs.
- Clusters of one-sentence paragraphs.
- Paragraphs that shift topic midstream.
- Loose sentences added because they are interesting.

## Information Order

Move:

- General before specific when entering a topic.
- Known before new within sentences and paragraphs.
- Aim before result when needed for interpretation.
- Result before implication.
- Limitation before future work.

Do not jump from broad context directly to technical detail without a bridge.

## Narrative Wrap

Data and information do not explain their own function. Add language that tells the reader why information is there and how it relates to the argument.

Use comments on information when needed:

- This pattern indicates...
- This comparison suggests...
- This limitation affects...
- Together, these findings support...

Keep comments proportional to evidence.
