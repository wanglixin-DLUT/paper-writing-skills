# Grammar And Vocabulary

Use this reference for language choices that affect scientific meaning.

## Verb Tense

Check whether tense communicates the intended meaning:

- Present simple: established knowledge, current facts, model behavior, paper structure.
- Present perfect: recent or continuing relevance, work completed with present significance.
- Past simple: what was done or found in a specific study.

Tense can change whether a result appears to be a study-specific finding or a general truth.

## Active And Passive

Passive voice is acceptable when it matches target convention, but check ownership.

Use active voice or explicit subjects when needed:

- we
- this study
- this paper
- our model
- the proposed method
- the experiment

Avoid ambiguity where the reader cannot tell whether the action was done by the authors, prior researchers, or a standard procedure.

## Terminology Stability

Repeat the same term for the same concept unless there is a clear reason to change.

Watch for drift:

- method / framework / model / approach / system
- dataset / corpus / benchmark / sample
- accuracy / performance / effectiveness
- finding / result / observation / evidence

Loose synonym replacement can make concepts appear unstable.

## Vocabulary Accuracy

Do not choose words for elegance if they change meaning. Prefer accurate, conventional words over varied wording.

Check:

- claim strength verbs
- causal verbs
- comparison adjectives
- novelty language
- limitation language

## Citations

Place citations where they show which information they support. Avoid stacking citations at the end of a sentence if different clauses come from different sources.

Use placeholders rather than invented citations.

## Articles And Number

Check a/an/the and singular/plural when they affect meaning:

- a model versus the model
- a limitation versus the limitation
- results show versus result shows
- data are versus data is, depending on target convention

Do not overfocus on minor grammar when larger meaning issues remain unresolved.
