# Keyword And Grammar Checks

Use this reference to make titles discoverable and readable.

## Keyword Strategy

Compare:

- Title keywords.
- Journal keyword list.
- Target-reader search terms.
- Method/model/dataset/task names.

The title and keyword list should complement each other. Do not duplicate every title word in the keyword list. Use keywords to carry broader or adjacent discoverability terms that would overload the title.

## Title Overload

Watch for:

- Too many nouns in a row.
- Multiple prepositional phrases.
- Several slash-separated concepts.
- Acronyms plus technical nouns.
- Too many datasets, metrics, or model components.

Fix by:

- Moving secondary terms to the keyword list.
- Using a colon if target journals do so.
- Replacing prepositional chains with clearer content words.
- Choosing one central focus.

## Grammar Checks

Ask:

- What is the grammatical head of the title?
- Does each modifier clearly attach to the right noun?
- Are prepositions unambiguous?
- Does a sentence title have a clear subject and verb?
- Does the title make sense to readers outside the immediate project team?

## Acronyms

Check:

- Is the acronym widely recognized by target readers?
- Is it already in target article titles?
- Could it mean something else in adjacent fields?
- Would the full term improve discoverability?

## Search Check

When the user asks for search-readiness, suggest testing the title and keyword set in scholarly search engines. Do not claim actual search results unless you have performed a user-approved search.
