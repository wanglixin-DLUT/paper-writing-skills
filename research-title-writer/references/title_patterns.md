# Title Patterns

Use these patterns as starting points and adapt to target-journal conventions.

## Method Or Tool

- `<Method name>: <descriptive function for target task>`
- `<Technical object> for <specific task or application>`
- `<Approach type> enables <capability> in <domain>`
- `<Model/component> improves <specific outcome> for <task>`

## Finding

- `<Main finding> in <system/dataset/domain>`
- `<Condition or mechanism> drives <observed outcome>`
- `<Intervention or method> reduces/increases <measured outcome>`

Use finding titles only when evidence is strong and target journals accept sentence-like titles.

## Dataset Or Benchmark

- `<Dataset/resource name>: <scope and purpose>`
- `<Benchmark> for evaluating <capability> in <domain>`
- `<Resource type> reveals <pattern or limitation>`

## Application

- `<Method or model> for <application> under <constraint>`
- `<Approach> supports <decision/use case> in <setting>`
- `<System> enables <practical capability> for <target users/context>`

## Comparison Or Evaluation

- `Evaluation of <method family> for <task> across <datasets/settings>`
- `<Proposed method> outperforms <baseline family> for <metric/task>`

Use performance verbs only when Results support them and comparison is fair.

## Colon Titles

Use a colon when target titles often split:

- Offer/name before colon; explanatory scope after colon.
- Broad topic before colon; specific contribution after colon.
- Method name before colon; task/application after colon.

Avoid colons when the result feels like two titles glued together.

## Variant Set

When asked for title options, provide:

- Technical title.
- Broad-reader title.
- Contribution-focused title.
- Conservative title.
- Short title.

Explain the tradeoff briefly.
