# Title Strategy

Use this reference before generating title options.

## Inputs

- Target journal or venue.
- Target reader.
- Central contribution.
- Method/model/dataset/task names.
- Key result or application.
- Keyword list, if available.
- Target-paper title examples.

## Target-Article Checks

If target titles are available, check:

- Average word count.
- Whether titles use colons.
- Whether titles are phrase titles or sentence titles.
- Whether acronyms appear.
- Whether method names, organism/dataset names, application terms, or result claims appear.
- Whether titles emphasize method, finding, task, or application.

## Scope Decision

Choose one dominant focus:

- Method/tool title: best when the method is the contribution.
- Finding title: best when the result is strong and target papers use sentence titles.
- Application title: best when the value is practical or translational.
- Dataset/benchmark title: best when a resource is the contribution.
- Mechanism title: best when the study explains how something works.
- Comparison title: best when performance against baselines is central.

## Acronym Decision

Use an acronym in the title only when:

- Target readers will know it.
- Target titles commonly use it.
- The full term would make the title unwieldy.
- The acronym does not block interdisciplinary discoverability.

Avoid acronyms when the title must attract a broader readership.

## Claim Scope

Check that the title does not imply:

- Broader datasets or populations than tested.
- Causality not established.
- Clinical or real-world deployment not evaluated.
- State-of-the-art performance without complete comparison.
- A general method when only one case or domain was tested.
