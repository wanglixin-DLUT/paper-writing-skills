# Research Title Writer

Use this skill to create or revise a scientific title that attracts the right readers and accurately predicts the paper's content.

This skill is part of the writing system:

- `content_analysis.md` supplies technical facts, method/model names, contribution evidence, and claim boundaries.
- `writing_blueprint.md` supplies target journal conventions, target reader, central contribution, terminology, and title-content expectations.

## Core Principle

A title is the paper's first and longest-lived entry point. It should be discoverable, readable, grammatically coherent, and honest about what the paper delivers.

Do not treat the working title as final. Do not overload the title with every keyword. The title must set expectations that the paper fulfills.

## Start Here

1. Look for `writing_blueprint.md` and `content_analysis.md` in the active project/manuscript directory and nearby parent directories.
2. If `writing_blueprint.md` exists, use it for:
   - target journal title conventions
   - target reader and keyword scope
   - central contribution
   - stable terminology and allowed acronyms
3. If `content_analysis.md` exists, use it for:
   - exact method/model/dataset/task names
   - evidence-supported contribution
   - claim boundaries
4. If target titles or journal requirements are provided, model length, structure, and acronym use from them.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose the task.
   - New title, title variants, shortening, grammar cleanup, keyword alignment, acronym decision, or title-content audit.

2. Build title constraints.
   - Use `references/title_strategy.md`.
   - Determine target length, structure, acronym tolerance, colon use, sentence-title tolerance, and keyword breadth.

3. Generate or revise titles.
   - Use `references/title_patterns.md`.
   - Produce variants that emphasize contribution, method, application, dataset, mechanism, or finding as appropriate.

4. Check discoverability and readability.
   - Use `references/keyword_and_grammar_checks.md`.
   - Balance title keywords with journal keyword list.
   - Reduce noun pileups and ambiguous prepositional chains.

5. Check promise accuracy.
   - Use `references/title_revision_checklist.md`.
   - Ensure title claims are supported by the manuscript and upstream artifacts.

## Output Modes

- `variants`: produce several title options with rationale.
- `revision`: improve an existing title.
- `shorten`: reduce length while preserving focus.
- `diagnosis`: identify title problems.
- `keyword alignment`: pair title and keyword list.
- `final check`: audit grammar, acronyms, and title-content match.

## Output Standards

A good title:

- Signals the central contribution or focus.
- Attracts the intended reader.
- Uses field-appropriate keywords.
- Is grammatically clear.
- Avoids unnecessary acronyms.
- Avoids overloaded noun strings.
- Does not promise stronger results, broader scope, or applications than the paper supports.
- Aligns with `content_analysis.md`, `writing_blueprint.md`, Abstract, Results, and Discussion.
