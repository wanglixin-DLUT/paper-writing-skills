# Abstract Revision Checklist

Use this checklist for drafts, compressions, and final pre-submission review.

## Fit

- Does the abstract follow journal word limit and heading requirements?
- Does it match the article type?
- Does it align with target-paper conventions from `writing_blueprint.md`?
- Does it align with technical facts and evidence from `content_analysis.md`, if available?
- If highlights or significance statements exist, does the abstract avoid unnecessary repetition?

## Standalone Clarity

- Can a reader understand the study without reading the paper?
- Is the central contribution explicit?
- Is the problem or gap clear enough to justify the study?
- Does the first sentence help the intended reader enter the topic?
- Does the final sentence leave a clear take-home message?

## Contribution Ownership

- Is it clear what the authors did, found, or provide?
- Could present simple tense make a new result look like established knowledge?
- Could passive voice hide the user's contribution?
- Would adding a phrase such as `this study`, `we`, `here`, or `the present work` remove ambiguity?

## Method And Result Selection

- Are method details included because they affect relevance, credibility, or novelty?
- Is the key result present?
- Are numbers included when they are needed to support the claim?
- Are secondary results removed unless they change the takeaway?
- Is the abstract free of details that belong only in Methods or Results?

## Claims And Certainty

- Does every implication follow from the reported result?
- Are early-stage or indirect implications marked as possible, potential, may, might, or could?
- Are strong verbs such as demonstrates, establishes, solves, or enables justified?
- Is the abstract consistent with limitations discussed in the paper?
- Would a reviewer identify any sentence as overclaiming?

## Terminology And Acronyms

- Is the same concept named consistently throughout?
- Are method, dataset, model, material, intervention, and outcome labels stable?
- Are acronyms necessary in the abstract?
- Is each necessary acronym defined once?
- Are there unexplained specialist terms that would block the intended reader?

## Sentence Flow

- Does each sentence follow logically from the previous one?
- Are sentence links explicit enough despite compression?
- Are `this`, `it`, `they`, and `which` attached to unmistakable referents?
- Are sentence lengths manageable?
- Are there stacked noun phrases that can be simplified?

## Compression

Cut in this order:

1. Generic field praise.
2. Repeated background.
3. Procedural method detail.
4. Secondary findings.
5. Redundant modifiers.
6. Phrases that state the obvious, such as `the results of this study`.

Protect:

- Central contribution.
- Key result.
- Required method detail.
- Claim boundaries.
- Terminology consistency.

## Final Output Check

Before delivering:

- Provide word count.
- Note any assumptions.
- Flag missing information that prevents a stronger version.
- If useful, provide a one-line move map showing the function of each sentence.
