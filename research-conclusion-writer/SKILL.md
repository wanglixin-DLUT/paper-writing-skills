# Research Conclusion Writer

Use this skill to write or revise a Conclusion that closes the paper clearly without becoming a second abstract or an unsupported promotional ending.

This skill is part of the writing system:

- `content_analysis.md` supplies technical facts, contribution evidence, limitations, and claim boundaries.
- `writing_blueprint.md` supplies target-journal conventions, conclusion length, move map, and final-message strategy.
- Results and Discussion supply the evidence and interpretations that can be safely carried into the ending.

## Core Principle

A Conclusion should make the study's contribution easy to identify for readers who may jump directly from the title or abstract. It should consolidate what the paper achieved, why it matters, and what remains bounded or future-facing.

Be explicit about the user's contribution. Do not assume the reader remembers earlier sections.

## Start Here

1. Look for `content_analysis.md`, `writing_blueprint.md`, and current Results/Discussion drafts in the active project/manuscript directory and nearby parent directories.
2. If `writing_blueprint.md` exists, use it for target conventions: separate Conclusion, final Discussion paragraph, word range, and expected moves.
3. If `content_analysis.md` exists, use it for contribution evidence, limitations, applications, and unsupported claims.
4. If Discussion does not exist, draft cautiously from Results and upstream artifacts.
5. If the journal has no separate Conclusion, produce a final Discussion paragraph instead.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose the task.
   - Separate Conclusion, final paragraph, conclusion revision, shortening, contribution sharpening, or future-work ending.

2. Select the conclusion structure.
   - Use `references/conclusion_models.md`.
   - Choose which moves are needed: paper content, achievement, method, key results, implications, limitations, applications, knowledge advance, future directions.

3. Build the final-message map.
   - Use `references/contribution_and_ending.md`.
   - Identify the one contribution the reader should remember.
   - Link it to evidence and boundaries.

4. Draft or revise.
   - Use `references/conclusion_workflow.md`.
   - Be explicit about what this study did or achieved.
   - Avoid over-detailed methods or result repetition.
   - End with contribution, application, or future direction depending on target convention.

5. Check ownership and claim strength.
   - Use `references/conclusion_revision_checklist.md`.
   - Ensure the conclusion does not sound like background knowledge or someone else's contribution.

## Output Modes

- `draft`: produce a complete Conclusion.
- `final paragraph`: produce a closing paragraph for Discussion.
- `revision`: rewrite or restructure an existing Conclusion.
- `compression`: shorten to a target word count.
- `contribution ending`: sharpen the final take-home message.
- `handoff`: produce a conclusion plan for later drafting.

## Output Standards

A good Conclusion:

- Clearly identifies the study's achievement or contribution.
- Synthesizes key results without relisting all results.
- States implications or applications proportionally.
- Acknowledges limitations or future work when target conventions support it.
- Uses tense and subjects that preserve contribution ownership.
- Aligns with `content_analysis.md`, `writing_blueprint.md`, Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion.
- Avoids new evidence, unsupported claims, and vague final sentences.
