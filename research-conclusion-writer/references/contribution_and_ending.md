# Contribution And Ending

Use this reference to keep the final message clear and defensible.

## Contribution Ownership

Readers may jump directly to the Conclusion. Make it clear what the current study did or found.

Strategies:

- Use `this study`, `this paper`, `we`, `our results`, `the present work`, or another explicit subject when needed.
- Use present perfect or past tense for work completed in the study.
- Avoid sentences that could be mistaken for background knowledge.

## Achievement Versus Contribution

- Achievement: what the study accomplished internally.
- Contribution: how that achievement changes knowledge, capability, method, evidence, or application.

Example pattern:

- Achievement: the study developed or evaluated a method.
- Contribution: the method enables a comparison, measurement, prediction, or interpretation not previously available under the same conditions.

## Ending Types

Choose one:

- Knowledge advance: best for theory, mechanism, or understanding.
- Methodological value: best for methods, tools, models, protocols, or datasets.
- Practical application: best for translational, clinical, engineering, or deployment studies.
- Future direction: best when evidence is promising but bounded.
- Limitation-aware close: best when constraints shape interpretation.

## Avoid Weak Endings

Avoid:

- "More research is needed" without a specific direction.
- Repeating the abstract's final sentence.
- Adding a new claim.
- Ending on a limitation without recovering the supported value.
- Using grand claims not supported by Results or Discussion.

## Claim Calibration

Before finalizing, ask:

- What can the reader safely remember as the paper's contribution?
- What evidence supports it?
- What scope limitation must travel with it?
- What wording preserves ownership?
